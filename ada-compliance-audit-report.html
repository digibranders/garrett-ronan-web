Internal Engineering Report

ADA Compliance & WCAG 2.1 Audit

Project: GKR Hospitality Web (2025)

Executive Summary

This report documents the current accessibility status of the GKR Hospitality website. As a premium hospitality firm operating in the US, compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) is legally required. The site was audited against WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards.

Current Status: Non-Compliant. While the semantic structure is strong, there are critical failures in color contrast and keyboard navigation that expose the organization to legal risks and prevent users with certain disabilities from accessing content.

1. Regulatory Context

In the United States, ADA Title III applies to all "places of public accommodation." The DOJ and US courts have interpreted this to include public-facing websites. Failure to comply can lead to lawsuits, which have increased by over 200% in the hospitality sector since 2021.

WCAG 2.1 Level A Partial Minimum baseline accessibility.
WCAG 2.1 Level AA Fail Standard legal benchmark for US businesses.
WCAG 2.2 Incomplete Latest recommendations involving mobile/cognitive aids.

2. Critical Findings (Priority 1)

Critical Failure

Insufficient Color Contrast

Location: LogosSection.tsx, PhilosophySection.tsx, FAQ (Contact Page)

The site uses a brand gold color (#c5a059) on a light cream background (#FFF7F2). This results in a contrast ratio of 2.18:1. The WCAG AA minimum is 4.5:1.

Effect: Text is unreadable for users with low vision or color blindness.

Critical Failure

Keyboard Trap / Navigation Inoperability

Location: gallery/page.tsx

The Portfolio/Gallery items are implemented as clickable <div> elements without tabindex. They cannot be reached or activated using the keyboard only.

Effect: Users who use screen readers or switches instead of a mouse are completely locked out of the "Selected Works" content.

Critical Failure

Missing Button Labels

Location: Navbar.tsx, gallery/page.tsx (Close icons)

The Mobile Hamburger menu and modal "X" close buttons use raw icons without text or aria-label.

Effect: Screen readers will announce "Button" with no context, providing zero utility to blind users.

3. Moderate Findings (Priority 2)

Moderate

Vague Imagery (Alt Text)

Location: Site-wide <Image /> tags.

Alt text currently uses placeholders like "Hero 1" or decorative terms. Meaningful images lack descriptive text, while strictly decorative images lack appropriate null alt tags.

Moderate

Motion Control (Vestibular Disorders)

Location: MovingRibbon.tsx

The site contains an infinitely looping high-motion ribbon at the top of every page. There is no mechanism to pause it.

Effect: Can cause discomfort or seizures for users with vestibular or motion sensitivities.

4. Recommendations & Roadmap

As per CTO standards, we recommend a two-phase remediation plan:

Phase 1: Legal Risk Mitigation (Immediate)

  • Update tailwind.config.ts to include an "Accessible Gold" with darkened contrast.
  • Apply aria-label to all iconic buttons.
  • Convert Gallery <div> wrappers to <button> or add ARIA roles/tabindex.

Phase 2: Semantic Cleanup (Following Sprints)

  • Implement aria-expanded states for FAQ accordion logic.
  • Conduct a full Alt-Text audit for all assets.
  • Add a "Reduced Motion" preference check or a pause button for moving components.
